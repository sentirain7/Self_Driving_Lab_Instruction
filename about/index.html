MATERIALS MECHANICS AND MULTIPHYSICS SIMULATION

Taeyoung Yun 윤태영

  • Research Fellow, Korea Institute of Civil Engineering and Building Technology (KICT), 2009–present
  • Full Professor (concurrent appointment), University of Science and Technology (UST), 2025–present

Research Fellow in computational materials mechanics, with research spanning viscoelasticity, viscoplasticity, continuum damage, and molecular interactions. Develops constitutive models and scientific software, verifies numerical implementations, and evaluates simulations against experiments. Experience includes finite element software development, molecular simulation of asphalt binders and polymer units, and simulation-based support for industrial equipment design.

Core Expertise

  • Deformable body mechanics — time-dependent deformation, rate-dependent hardening and softening, cohesive viscoelastic contact, and continuum damage modeling.
  • Finite element and multiphysics analysis — in-house FEA development, Abaqus, coupled CFD–DEM, heat and mass transfer, and Ansys Fluent user-defined functions.
  • Molecular structure and properties — MD of organic–inorganic systems, polymer units in asphalt binder, tensile response, and moisture- and salt-water-induced degradation; LAMMPS and Materials Studio/Forcite.
  • Scientific programming and materials informatics — Python, C++, Fortran, GNN-based supergraph models (Graphormer3D), tabular models (Random Forest and XGBoost), automated simulation workflows, and validation of predictive models.

Selected Research and Engineering Contributions

Constitutive Modeling and Finite Element Analysis

  • Contributed to VEPCD-FEP++, a multiaxial viscoelastoplastic continuum damage finite element program, within a US Federal Highway Administration research program (2005–2008). Related work addressed performance specifications based on viscoelastoplastic continuum damage models (2008).
  • Modeled rate-dependent viscoplastic hardening and softening of asphalt mixtures in compression, addressing time-dependent mechanical behavior under loading.
  • Developed in-house finite element analysis code in Fortran for structural simulation, including a load discretization algorithm for jointed concrete pavement.
  • Implemented a linear cohesive viscoelastic contact model in C++ for DEM and verified its response against analytical solutions. Extended EDEM contact models for particle–solid interactions and evaluated models through experimental validation.
  • Evaluated similarity principles and exact scaling in DEM using analytical solutions, examining the effects of particle dynamics, contact models, and geometry on the validity of scaled simulations.

Molecular Simulation of Binder Materials and Interfaces

  • Investigated material properties of polymer units in asphalt binder using molecular dynamics and machine learning.
  • Simulated amorphous–crystalline interactions and tensile response in organic–inorganic material systems. Related published research examined moisture- and salt-water-induced degradation of asphalt concrete mixtures.
  • Prepared a 5,346-case Materials Studio/Forcite MD dataset for density and energy-density prediction. Developed a GNN-based supergraph model using an adapted Graphormer3D architecture, integrating molecular, interaction, and system features.
  • Evaluated supergraph model generalization on 891 held-out cases, benchmarked against tabular models (Random Forest and XGBoost), and performed feature-ablation studies. Assessed representative molecular-system size using density and radial distribution functions.
  • Co-authored a study using large-scale ReaxFF reactive molecular dynamics to investigate early-stage hydration of four cement clinker phases under temperature-accelerated conditions, relating crystal structure to phase-specific reaction mechanisms (Construction and Building Materials, 2026).

Multiphysics Analysis and Industrial Design Support

  • Coupled DEM and CFD to investigate particle transport, clogging, and pavement permeability with experimental validation. Also modeled airflow-driven cooling of asphalt mixtures during transport.
  • Modeled aggregate transport in rotating and double-drum equipment using engineering design drawings. Evaluated residence time and velocity to inform design decisions for a Korean plant equipment company.
  • Developed a cohesive shrinking particle model for soil drying in DEM, incorporating moisture-dependent cohesion and particle size. Compared changes in specimen radius and thickness with published experiments and assessed limitations in curling and crack development.

Simulation Automation and Digital Research Workflows

  • Developed COMID to automate molecular bulk-system preparation and equilibration, crystalline and multilayer structure generation, and tensile simulations under varied conditions. The frontend and backend contain over 140,000 lines of Python code.
  • Designed local queueing and an SSH remote-compute bridge. Executed over 2,000 LAMMPS conditions on six H200 GPUs using automated queueing and CUDA MPS.
  • Developed early-stage self-driving laboratory workflows and reusable MD analysis procedures, providing a basis for linking physics-based models, experimental validation, and automated analysis in future digital-twin applications.
